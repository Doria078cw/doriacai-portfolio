Background

Hear Your Sound explores how an interactive experience might bring attention back to the moving body. Drawing on embodiment as a design perspective, the project turns movement into something participants can hear and adjust.

Mechanism

The concept treats the body as a collection of instruments. Instrument-inspired visual forms connect bodily movement with qualities of sound, while the working experiments focus on mapping movement to musical pitch.

Interaction paradigm

A camera captures movement, pose estimation extracts body keypoints, and mapping rules translate those measurements into notes. Participants move, listen to the response, and adjust their gestures, creating a continuous loop of movement and auditory feedback.

Prototype & iteration

I first generated notes from simple waveforms, then connected OpenPose tracking to pitch. Absolute hand positions were difficult to anchor, so I shifted to relative body points. Leg mappings also needed revision because camera distance affected the measurements; I tested changes between joint positions instead.

Experience

The intended experience is open-ended: participants explore how changes in posture and movement affect the sound they produce. The scenario below presents the body as both the instrument and the interface; the prototype demonstrates the mapping approach rather than a measured improvement in body awareness.
