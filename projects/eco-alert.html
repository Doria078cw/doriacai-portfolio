Background

Air pollution can feel remote even when people know it is harmful. A questionnaire with 30 respondents and follow-up interviews informed the project’s focus: making environmental harm perceptible through the experience of a plant.

Mechanism

Players explore a virtual environment and touch a plant to take on its sensory perspective. Visualized air particles and physical feedback connect pollution in the scene with sensations such as heat, airflow, mist, and pressure. The design combines free exploration with moments of embodiment.

Interaction paradigm

Touch initiates a shift from observing a plant to experiencing its surroundings. A breathing belt maps the player’s breathing to bubbles that represent gas exchange; contact with air particles changes their appearance. VR visuals and the wearable device create a feedback loop between the player’s body and the environment.

Physical prototyping

We tested atomizer and airbag components before combining them with the breathing belt and other feedback modules. Iteration focused on wearability, contact with the arm, and reliable operation as a linked device.

Implementation & feedback

The prototype connects particle-based plant and pollution visuals with breathing input and Arduino feedback. Early participants responded to the unfamiliar perspective, while hardware weight limited comfort. The next design priorities were a lighter enclosure, more stable connections, and a broader range of scenes.
