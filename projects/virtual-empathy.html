Virtual Empathy

A VR serious game exploring how teenage bystanders respond to cyberbullying.

Background

Cyberbullying involves victims, bullies, and bystanders. Virtual Empathy focuses on the bystander: how can an interactive experience help young people consider the consequences of remaining silent and explore ways to respond?

Research & design direction

The team’s questionnaire and interviews with five teenagers pointed to uncertainty about intervening, fear of becoming a target, and difficulty understanding a victim’s experience. These findings informed a design built around perspective-taking, choices, and guided reflection.

Mechanism

Players move between a bystander’s room and a space representing the victim’s experience. Their responses to comments influence the subsequent experience. The game sits within a proposed workshop flow that includes preparation, VR participation, and discussion.

Interaction paradigm

The interaction alternates between making a social choice and experiencing its consequences from another perspective. Players inspect comments, choose a response, enter the victim’s space, and return to reconsider their next action. The storyboard makes this repeated choice-and-feedback loop explicit.

Prototype & workshop

I implemented movement, direct and ray interactions, visual effects, and scene transitions. A youth-center workshop generated feedback about the intensity of the effects and the clarity of the choices. The pilot did not include a controlled comparison or professional assessment of empathy change, so it offers formative feedback rather than evidence of lasting impact.
